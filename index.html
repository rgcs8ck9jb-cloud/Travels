The walk, west to east

The route runs from the oldest building in Central Asia to the last emir's world, in roughly the order Bukhara was built. It ends at Lyabi Hauz, which is where you want to be having dinner.

Doable in one long day, but in August it is better as two half-days: stops 1–7 in the morning, 8–19 from late afternoon into the evening.

Worth a taxi

Three sites outside the walkable centre. If you only do one, make it Sitorai Mokhi-Khosa — it is the only place that shows you the emirate's final, strangest chapter.

Taxis are cheap and everywhere; Yandex Go works in Bukhara and removes the haggling.

August: plan around the heat

  • Expect 38–42 °C in the afternoon, and very little shade at the Ark, Poi Kalyan and the Registan.
  • Walk 07:00–11:30, stop, then resume 17:00 until dark. The monuments are floodlit in the evening and half-empty.
  • Carry 2 litres of water per person and refill — shops everywhere, roughly 3–5,000 som a bottle.
  • Hats and sunscreen for all three of you. The heat is dry, so you dehydrate without noticing you are sweating.

Suggested timing (two half-days)

  • 07:00 Stops 1–3 · Samanid Mausoleum, Chashma Ayub, park
  • 08:30 Stops 4–6 · Bolo Hauz, the Ark, Zindan
  • 11:00 Stop 7 · Poi Kalyan, then lunch and a long break
  • 17:00 Stops 8–15 · madrasas, the four trading domes, Magoki Attori, Gaukushon
  • 19:00 Stop 16 · Lyabi Hauz for dinner under the mulberry trees
  • Day 2 am Stops 17–19 · Chor Minor, Jewish quarter, Khodjaev house
  • Day 2 pm Taxi to Sitorai Mokhi-Khosa and/or Chor Bakr at golden hour

Tickets & money

  • Most sites charge 20,000–30,000 som; the Ark is the expensive one at roughly 60,000 som for foreigners.
  • Prices rise most years and the figures on this page are indicative. Verify at the ticket window.
  • Bring cash in som. Cards work in hotels and better restaurants, patchily at ticket booths.
  • ATMs cluster around Lyabi Hauz. Some charge; Kapitalbank and Ipoteka generally accept foreign cards.
  • A photo permit is occasionally an extra few thousand som. Tripods usually need permission.
  • Budget roughly 250–350,000 som per adult for all the entries on this page.

Dress & etiquette

  • Shoulders and knees covered at all religious sites. A light scarf for your wife at Bolo Hauz, the Kalyan Mosque and — non-negotiable — at Bahauddin Naqshband.
  • Shoes off inside working mosques. Slip-ons save time.
  • Mir-i-Arab is a functioning seminary: view from the portal, do not enter, keep voices down.
  • Ask before photographing people, especially at prayer and in the Jewish quarter.
  • Bargaining in the trading domes is expected and friendly. Open at about half, settle near two-thirds.

With a child

  • Cobbles, uneven brick and the Ark's steep entry ramp — a carrier beats a stroller across most of this route.
  • Best bits for a child: climbing onto the Chor Minor roof, the donkey statue at Lyabi Hauz, the water tower lift, counting brick patterns at the Samanid Mausoleum, and the bird-and-dragon hunt on the Abdulaziz Khan facade.
  • Consider skipping the Zindan prison (stop 6) — pits and mannequins.
  • Samani Park at stop 1 has shade and a small funfair; the Lyabi Hauz chaikhanas are relaxed about children.

Eating and resting on the route

  • Chasmai Mirob — terrace above Poi Kalyan, the classic view with a cold drink. Near stop 7.
  • Lyabi Hauz chaikhanas — plov, shashlik, tea under the trees. The natural end of the walk.
  • Old Bukhara / Silk Road Teahouse — near Lyabi Hauz, reliable, English menus.
  • Bukhara Water Tower café — stop 3, mainly for the view.
  • Try bakhsh (green herb rice) and oshi savo — both Bukharan Jewish dishes that became city standards.
